Lightning lives here

South Mississippi is one of the most lightning-active regions in the country, and summer thunderstorm season runs for months. But lightning isn't the only threat — utility switching, downed lines being re-energized, and even your own AC compressor cycling can send spikes through your wiring.

Most homeowners don't think about surges until the $400 HVAC board dies the week after a storm. By then the damage is done across half the house. Protection is cheap. Replacement isn't.

How whole-home protection works

A Type 1 or Type 2 surge protective device (SPD) mounts right at your electrical panel — the single choke point every circuit in your home passes through. When voltage spikes above safe levels, the SPD shunts the excess to ground in nanoseconds, clamping the surge before it reaches your outlets.

Think of it like a bouncer at the front door: normal power walks right in, a 6,000-volt spike gets thrown to the curb. The best units have indicator lights so you can see at a glance they're still protecting you — and we check them on every service visit.

What it actually protects

Everything with a circuit board — which in a modern home is nearly everything:

  • HVAC systems — control boards run $300–600 plus the service call.
  • Refrigerators, washers, dryers — appliance boards are surge candy.
  • TVs, computers, routers — the obvious ones, and the expensive ones.
  • Garage door openers, smart thermostats, doorbells — the stuff you forget until it dies.
  • EV chargers — a $500+ wall unit you really don't want to replace.

A plug-in power strip protects one outlet. A whole-home SPD protects every outlet in the house — including the hardwired stuff no strip can reach.

One device, every outlet

Install takes under two hours: we mount the SPD at your panel, wire it to a dedicated breaker, verify the ground path (critical — a surge protector is only as good as its ground), and test the indicators. For layered defense we can add point-of-use protection at your most sensitive gear too.
