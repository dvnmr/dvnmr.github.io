YOUR PANEL IS TALKING.

Signs your panel needs an upgrade — the warnings that mean "soon," the ones that mean "now," and the one that means stop reading and call.

Your electrical panel is the one part of your house that's supposed to complain before things get dangerous. Breakers trip to protect you. The problem starts when the complaining becomes the routine — or when the panel stops being able to complain at all. Here are the signs I take seriously, in the order I take them.

Call now: the emergency signs

  • Burning smell from the panel — like hot plastic or electrical burning. Don't reset anything. Don't "keep an eye on it." That's heat where heat should never be, and it's how electrical fires start.
  • Buzzing, crackling, or sizzling from inside the panel — that's arcing: electricity jumping a gap it shouldn't jump, cooking everything around the jump.
  • Scorch marks, melted insulation, or a panel that's warm to the touch. A panel should be room temperature and boring. Anything else is a failure in progress.

Fire, smoke, active arcing, or immediate danger? Keep clear and call 911. For utility outages or damaged service lines, contact the utility. Moore Electric does not offer emergency panel, service, or rewiring work.

The "soon" signs: your panel is out of room

  • Breakers that trip regularly. A breaker that trips once when you ran the microwave and the space heater together did its job. Breakers that trip weekly are telling you the circuit — or the panel — is overloaded or worn out. And a breaker you have to reset constantly gets weak, which makes it trip more, which is not a spiral you win.
  • Lights that flicker or dim when the AC kicks on. A brief dip when a big motor starts can be normal. Whole-house flicker, lights that never quite stay bright, or dimming that gets worse over time points at capacity or connection problems upstream.
  • You still have a fuse box. Fuses were fine in their era. That era assumed a house with a fraction of today's electrical load — and the classic fuse-box failure is somebody screwing in a bigger fuse to stop the nuisance blows, which quietly removes the protection entirely.
  • 100-amp service running a 2026 house. Plenty of older Gulf Coast homes were built with 100-amp service. Add central AC, an electric range and dryer, a water heater, and then ask it for a hot tub, a workshop, or an EV charger, and the math stops working. 200-amp service is the modern standard for a reason.
  • No empty breaker spaces. If every slot is full — or doubled up with tandem breakers stuffed where they don't belong — there's nowhere for the next circuit to go, and that next circuit always comes.
  • Two-prong outlets everywhere. A house full of ungrounded outlets is a house wired before modern loads and modern safety standards. Not a panel problem by itself, but it tells you the age of everything behind the walls.

The age problem

Panels wear out. Breakers are mechanical devices, and most are at the end of their reliable life somewhere in the 25-to-40-year range. Worse, some panel brands from decades past are notorious for breakers that fail to trip at all when they should — the one failure you can't afford, because a breaker that won't trip hands the job to your wiring, and wiring handles it by getting hot. If your panel is old enough that you don't know what's in it, that's reason enough to have it looked at. Insurance companies increasingly think so too — an aging or known-problem panel can complicate a policy or a home sale.

What an upgrade involves

A panel or service upgrade may involve replacement equipment, utility coordination, a permit, and inspection. The authorized contractor and utility establish the actual scope and outage schedule. Moore Electric does not offer panel or service upgrades.

Arrange an assessment with a contractor authorized for panel and service work. For fire, smoke, active arcing, or immediate danger, keep clear and call 911; contact the utility for damaged service lines.
